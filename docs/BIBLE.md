AFTERZERO: THE LAST VERSION OF EARTH
COMPLETE 25–30 HOUR GAMEPLAY SCRIPT
A cinematic temporal-reality thriller combining third-person action, stealth, open-region exploration, causal puzzles,
alternate histories, and emotionally consequential choices.



    FORMAT                                      CAMPAIGN TARGET                             STRUCTURE
    Single-player narrative action-adventure    25–30 hours                                 23 missions + optional cases


Design note: durations are targets, not measured playtest results. This document is a narrative/gameplay blueprint for production
planning; it does not represent a completed game.
 1. THE GAME AT A GLANCE




 Logline
 In 2049, temporal systems engineer Kai Varen activates a machine that lets him interact with different states of the
 same physical world. Searching for his sister, Mira, he discovers that every attempt to repair history has helped create
 a planetary catastrophe—and that the person guiding him may be the future version of himself.

 Creative pillars
 • Temporal cause and effect: actions in one era alter routes, evidence, hazards, factions, and character outcomes in
 another.

 • Mystery with fair clues: twists should feel surprising on first viewing and logical in hindsight.

 • Cinematic action with quieter investigation: combat, traversal, environmental puzzles, and character scenes
 alternate.

 • Consequences without punishment: major choices change missions and relationships but do not casually block the
 main campaign.

 • A personal ending: the final decision is about grief, identity, and the limits of control—not simply defeating a villain.

 Campaign timing
    Section                                    Target           Core experience

    Prologue — The Zero Second                 2h               First anomaly; tutorial; Chronolith

    Act I — The World That Remembers           5h               Discovery; ancient mystery; causal puzzles

    Act II — The Nine-Minute War               6h               Combat escalation; looping city; factions

    Act III — The Last Version of Earth        6h               Future exploration; consequences; Witness

    Act IV — The Unwritten Second              5h               Unreliable memory; reality-breaking puzzles

    Finale and epilogue                        1.5 h            Final choice; ending; sequel hook

 2. WORLD, CHARACTERS AND CORE SYSTEMS




 Main characters
 Kai Varen — protagonist. A temporal systems engineer who believes every event has a discoverable cause. His flaw
 is believing every loss can be reversed.

 Mira Varen — the impossible sister. Kai’s sister, officially dead for 23 years. She is a reconstructed consciousness
 carrying memories from more than one possible life.

 The Witness — older Kai. A future Kai who has repeatedly tried to save Mira, creating new timelines and guiding his
 younger self toward the same experiment.

 Sera Voss — resistance leader. A survivor from 2196 who distrusts Kai because he is remembered as the architect
 of the Fracture.

 Dr. Elias Rook. Director of the Helix project; hides the early history of the Chronolith.

 Niko. A young technician whose clues appear across multiple temporal states.

 Dr. Anja Vale. A future scientist who studies temporal memory and the human cost of erased timelines.

 The four temporal states
 • Past: reveals original structures, causes, and objects that no longer exist.

 • Present: supports investigation, modern machinery, grounded stealth, and human conflict.

 • Future: exposes consequences, enables traversal through ruins, and introduces unstable temporal zones.

 • Inverted state: temporarily reverses selected processes, subject to energy and stability limits.

 Signature systems
 • Temporal State Switching — move between authored states of one location; not unrestricted date selection.

 • Temporal Residue — inspect objects for visual/audio traces of previous events.

 • Causal Anchors — selected objects persist between states after a deliberate action.

 • Echo Step — brief afterimage that distracts enemies or crosses a dangerous gap; consumes stability.

 • Local Loops — observe repeated sequences and use knowledge to solve timing-based puzzles.

 • Persistent Consequences — large actions modify the condition of locations in later missions.

    Design rule: every major temporal puzzle must be solvable through observable evidence. The player
    may be surprised, but solutions must not feel arbitrary.

 3. PROLOGUE — THE ZERO SECOND (2 HOURS)




 MISSION 1: THE LAST NORMAL DAY
 Target duration: 35 minutes   Purpose: Introduce Kai and establish the first anomaly.

 Story setup — Kai commutes through a rain-soaked city to Helix Temporal Research Facility. Small impossible events
 occur around him: a toy jumps back into a child’s hand, a clock runs backward, and a camera displays tomorrow’s
 footage.

 Playable sequence
 • Explore the city and inspect optional anomalies.

 • Receive an emergency message from Dr. Rook.

 • Reach the facility through a crowded security checkpoint.

 • When all clocks stop, rescue trapped civilians as a train crashes into the platform.

    ““The event lasted one second.” / “What event?” / “That’s the problem. Every recording says it hasn’t
    happened yet.””

 Mission ending: A voice speaks through every speaker: “Kai Varen. Do not build the machine.” Kai sees a woman
 resembling Mira vanish into the crowd.


 MISSION 2: HELIX GOES DARK
 Target duration: 45 minutes   Purpose: Introduce stealth, residue investigation and the Chronolith.

 Story setup — The facility locks down. Rook calls the Zero Second a system failure but blocks access to the central
 chamber.

 Playable sequence
 • Navigate dark corridors using emergency lighting.

 • Avoid malfunctioning security drones.

 • Find three power cells and restore a lift.

 • Use recorded conversations to identify a trustworthy officer.

 • Fight a security unit that rewinds damage; lure it into a damaged power conduit.

    ““This drawing is twenty-three years old.” / “Then someone has been using your credentials.” / “I was
    twelve.””

 Mission ending: Kai finds a child’s drawing of himself and Mira beside a black circular machine, then enters the
 Chronolith chamber.

 MISSION 23: AFTERZERO

 The three endings
 A — The Fixed World
 Kai destroys the Chronolith. Temporal fractures close and history stabilizes, but Mira’s reconstructed existence cannot
 survive.

    “For the first time, tomorrow belonged to tomorrow.”

 B — The Infinite War
 Kai separates temporal states into independent branches. Mira survives, but civilizations begin fighting over the
 boundaries between timelines.

    “We had saved every possible tomorrow. None of them agreed on the future.”

 C — Afterzero (recommended canon)
 Kai becomes the anchor that absorbs contradictions. Mira wakes in a world where Kai was never born, with only a faint
 memory of a voice urging her to live.

    “I finally found a second that belongs to nobody.”

 10. PRODUCTION ROADMAP AND QUALITY BAR
 Build the smallest playable proof first. Do not attempt the full campaign before the core temporal system is proven.

 Milestone 1 — One room, three states
 Build a room that can switch between past, present and future. A change in one state must visibly affect the others.
 Measure switching performance, collision stability, lighting, and state persistence.

 Milestone 2 — 20–30 minute vertical slice
 Prototype “Helix Goes Dark”: movement, interaction, one stealth encounter, one causal puzzle, one character scene
 and the first Chronolith reveal. Use placeholder dialogue and assets until the gameplay loop works.

 Milestone 3 — First 1–2 hour chapter
 Complete the prologue and test whether players understand the Chronolith without lengthy tutorials. Observe where
 they get stuck and revise environmental clues.

 Milestone 4 — Campaign production
 Expand the tested systems across five acts. Reuse underlying technology, but ensure each location has its own visual
 identity and puzzle logic. Author temporal states deliberately rather than attempting unrestricted simulation of all
 history.

 Milestone 5 — Narrative and gameplay validation
 Measure real completion times, puzzle failure rates, combat difficulty, optional-content uptake, and whether the major
 twists make sense after the reveal. Playtest all endings for emotional clarity.

 Quality bar
 • Every state switch must be readable, responsive and reversible when the story permits.

 • Every critical puzzle must provide multiple clues and a recovery path.

 • Choices should have visible consequences, not only different dialogue lines.

 • The Witness’s identity and Mira’s status need foreshadowing without making the mystery obvious.

 • The final act must reward knowledge gathered across the whole campaign.

 • The game should not claim complete originality; distinctiveness must be demonstrated through the finished execution
 and tested player experience.




 THE DEFINING PLAYER MOMENT
    A player changes one small object in the past, sees the world transform in the present, and only later
    discovers that the change created the threat they are fighting. That moment should define
    AFTERZERO.

 Quest chain A — Last Train Home (Jun Park, Rain City; 35–50 minutes)
 Hook: A station announcement repeats every 41 seconds. Jun is trying to evacuate passengers, but a locked maintenance
 gate keeps resetting to its pre-disaster state. Steps: interview Jun; inspect the damaged relay in the present; switch to an
 earlier state and reroute power; return to the present; choose whether to use the repaired line for evacuation or divert power
 to a Helix evidence terminal. Outcome: saved passengers create a later safe-route encounter; the evidence option gives a
 stronger case against Rook. The main story remains completable either way.

   Sample dialogue:
   Jun: “The train keeps arriving. Nobody gets on. Nobody gets off. But I can hear them in the next carriage.”
   Kai: “They are not here now.”
   Jun: “Then why do I keep apologizing to them?”

 Quest chain B — The Water That Remembers (Tala and Niko, Ancient Settlement;

 clue, and a changed future garden.

   Tala: “You ask where the river began. We ask who taught it to stay.”

 • Critical clues are reinforced by at least two channels: spoken line plus environmental evidence, or archive plus observable
